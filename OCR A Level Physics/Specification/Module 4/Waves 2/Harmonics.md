## Stationary waves on strings
If a string is stretched between two fixed points, these points act as nodes. When the string is plucked a progressive wave travels along the string and reflects off its ends. This creates two progressive waves travelling in opposite directions that form a stationary wave.

When the string is plucked, it vibrates in its fundamental node of vibration. The wavelength of the progressive wave is double the length of the string. 

# Harmonics and Wavelength
The fundamental frequency $f_{0}$ is the minimum frequency of a stationary wave for a string. Along with this fundamental mode of vibration, the string can form other stationary waves called harmonics at higher frequencies. 

All other harmonics have a frequency that is a multiple of the fundamental frequency. 

For a given string with fixed tension, the wave speed of progressive waves is constant. Thus:
$$
\lambda \propto \frac{1}{f_{0}}
$$
If the stationary wave's frequency is $2f_{0}$ then the wavelength will be half $f_{0}$. Remember that at $f_{0}$, the wavelength is $2L$, therefore at $2f_{0}$, the wavelength will be $L$. 

This pattern can be extended to show that the wavelength of the progressive wave can be determine by:
$$
\lambda = \frac{2L}{n_{a}}
$$
where $n_{a}$ is the number of antinodes incident on the string.
