The Young Double Slit experiment uses two double slits to determine the wavelength of monochromatic light. 

Monochromatic light means only light with a specific wavelength is being emitted from the source. 

In Young's experiment, he devised a method to produce monochromatic light by placing a colour filter in front of the filament lamp. 

The light is then passed through a single slit. This is to ensure the light coming towards the double slits is coherent. This due to only diffracted light making it through single gap and diffracted waves all share the same speed, frequency and wavelength.

Each slit acts as a coherent source of waves. In doing so, this lead to the diffracted waves overlapping and constructively interfering to light and dark fringes of maxima and minima.
