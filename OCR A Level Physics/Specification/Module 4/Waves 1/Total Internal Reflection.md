Total internal reflections occurs at the boundary between two media.  When the angle of incidence is above a critical angle $C$, it is totallly internally reflected. All the light is reflected back into the original medium. 

The two conditions for TIR are
1. Light must be travelling in a higher refractive index between the two media
2. The angle of incidence between the boundary must be above the critical angle $C$. The angle $C$ depends on the refractive index of th medium

# Critical Angle
Using $n_{1}\sin\theta_{1}  = n_{2}\sin \theta_{2}$. You can determine the relationship between the refractive index of the medium and the critical angle when light travels from the medium into air. 

To determine the critical angle $C$, the value of $\theta_{2}$ will always be $90$. Therefore for any two media, the critical angle, $C$, for the first media $n_{1}$ can be found as:
$$
\begin{gather*}
n_{1}\sin C = n_{2}\sin 90  \\ \\
C = \arcsin\left(\frac{n_{2}\sin 90}{n_{1}}\right)
\end{gather*}
$$

The critical angle can be found experimentally also. This is done by rotating and measuring the incident ray until the angle of refraction is $90$.
