Displacement - The distance from the equilibrium position in a particular direction

Amplitude - The maximum displacement of a wave. 

Wavelength - The distance between two adjacent points on a wave that are oscillating in phase. It is also the distance between adjacent peaks or troughs. 

Period - The time taken for the wave to move a distance of one wavelength. The time taken make complete oscillation. 

Frequency - The number of wavelengths passing through a point per unit time. 

Wave speed - The distance travelled by the wave per unit time. 

## Frequency and period 
Below is the relationship between period T and frequency
$$
f= \frac{1}{T}
$$

## Wave equation 
The relationship between wave speed $v$, wavelength $\lambda$ and frequency is called the wavelength. The wave equation $v =  f\lambda$ can be used for any periodic wave.

## Proof
We know that $v = \frac{d}{t}$. In this case the distance travelled for a wave in one complete oscillation is one complete wavelength $\lambda$. The time taken to travel a complete oscillation is it's period. Thus $v = \frac{\lambda}{T}$. However $\frac{1}{T} = f$. Therefore $v = \lambda f$.

## Phase difference
If particles are oscillating perfectly in step with each other and reach their maximum positive displacement at the same time. They have a difference of zero. 

Particles oscillating completely out of step with each other, one reaches its maximum displacement at the same time the other reaches its negative displacement, they are in antiphase. They have a phase difference of $\pi$. 

Phase difference is given by:
$$
\phi = \frac{x}{\lambda} (360)
$$
