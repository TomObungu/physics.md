Speakers are amongst hundreds of devices that make use of a potential divider circuit. These circuits can very the p.d across an output when connected to a fixed input.

# Potential dividers
You may find you need a p.d of $V for a specific task, but the power source is a 9V battery. You can use potential dividers to divide the p.d to give any value you require up to the maximum supplied form the power source.

A a pair of resistors in series will divide the p.d across them, depending on the ratio of their resistance. 

![[Pasted image 20260103204238.png]]

The output p.d $V_{out}$ is taken across the resistor of resistance $R_{2}$ - this a fraction of the total p.d $V_{in}$ across the resistors. The bigger the resistance $R_{2}$, the larger $V_{out}$ is:

The output p.d is given by the equation:
$$
V_{out} = \left(\frac{R_{2}}{R_{1}+R_{2}} \right) \times V_{in}
$$
This relationship is often simply called the potential divider equation. 
![[Pasted image 20260103204439.png]]

The current in the resistors is the same therefore:
$$
I = \frac{V_{1}}{R_{1}} = \frac{V_{2}}{R_{2}}
$$
or $$
\frac{V_{1}}{V_{2}} = \frac{R_{1}}{R_{2}}
$$
