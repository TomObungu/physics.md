The photoelectric effect described the emission of electrons from the surface of a material after it interacts with electromagnetic radiation. 

## The gold-leaf electroscope
Briefly touching the top plate with the negative electrode from a high voltage supply will negatively charge the electroscope. Excess electrons build at the top of plate and are dispersed through the stem of electroscope and gold leaf. Due to the stem and the gold leaf having the same negative charge. The gold leaf repels against the stem and raises.
![[Pasted image 20260501173847.png]]

If UV radiation is incident on the metal plate, the gold leaf starts to fall back down towards the stem. This shows that the electroscope has slowly lost its negative charge. 


## Key observations from the photoelectric effect 

1. Photoelectrons were only emited if the UV radiation was above a certain threshold frequency. There this means the photoelectric effects only works for EM radiation with certain energies.
2. If the incident radiation was above the threshold frequency the emission of electrons was instantneous
3. Increasing the intensity of the threshold frequency did not increase the maximum kinetic energy of the electrons. It only increased the rate of emission of the electrons. The kinetic energy only increased in excess with the threshold frequency. 

## Quantum rules
- A single photons can only interacts with a single electron the of the metal 
- Energy is conserved
- An electron is only released when the photon energy is equal to the the work function $\phi$ of the metal 
- At threshold frequency $f_{0}$ of the metal is equal to $\phi$
- Intensity only affects rate of emission if and only if the incident radiation is above the threshold frequency
