Electromagnetic waves a dual nature. The travel as waves and interact as 'particle' (photons). Louis de Brogile, in 1923, proposed that matter, which includes particles such as electrons, must also have a dual nature. 

## De Brogile Equation
A particle travelling through space with a momentum $p$ has an associated wavelength $\lambda$ by the de Brogile equation. 
$$
h = \frac{\lambda}{p} \ \text{ or } \ \lambda = \frac{h}{mv}
$$
where $h$ is the Planck constant, $m$ is the mass of the particle, and $v$ is its speed. 

## Evidence 
The wave-like nature of electrons can be demonstrated using an electron diffraction tube. Accelerated electrons pass through a thin sheet of graphite. The separation between the atomic layers of graphite is very similar to the wavelength of an electron. Electrons are diffracted between the separations of the polycrystalite graphite and form diffraction rings. This therefore demonstrates the wave-like nature behaviour of particles.
