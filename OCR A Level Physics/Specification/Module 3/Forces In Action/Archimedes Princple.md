When underwater, you will experience pressure from the water and also and upwards force called upthrust.

# Derivation of $p = h\rho g$
When there is a column of fluid of height $h$ and cross-sectional area $A$. The density of the fluid is $\rho$.
![[Pasted image 20260101011823.png]]

Pressure at the base = weight of column/cross sectional area:
$$
\begin{gather*} 
\rho = \frac{m}{V} \\ 
\rho V = m \\ \\
p = \frac{mg}{A} \\
p = \frac{\rho Vg}{A} \\ 
p = \frac{\rho Ahg}{A} \\ \\ 
\therefore p = h\rho g
\end{gather*}
$$
The pressure $p$ exerted by the column is independent of its cross sectional-area:
$$
p \propto h
$$

The pressure in a fluid  has an unusual feature - it is the same in all direction at the current height.

# Archimedes' principle
An object submerged in a fluid experience an upward force. The pressure at the bottom of the surface of the object is greater than the pressure at its top surface.

Archimedes' principle states that the upthrust exerted on a body immersed in a fluid, whether fully or partially submerged, is equal to the weight of the fluid that body displaces.

Below is a block of wood submerged in fluid with density $\rho$. The block will displace fluid.
![[Pasted image 20260101012751.png]]

force at the top of the surface = $h\rho gA$

force at the bottom of the surface = $(h+x)\rho gA$

Thus the resultant force upwards = $(h+x-h)h\rho gA$ 

Therefore the resultant force is:
$$
\text{upthrust} = Ax\rho g
$$

The volume of the block of fluid displaced is a $Ax$ and it has mass $Ax\rho$. So the upthrust is equal to the weight $(Ax\rho g)$ of the fluid displaced by the block. 

An object will sink if the upthrust is less than the weight of the object.
