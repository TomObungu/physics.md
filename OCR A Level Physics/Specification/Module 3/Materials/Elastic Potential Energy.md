Energy is stored in a spring when it is extended or compressed elastically. T

The area under a force-extension graph is equal to the work done by the force:
![[Pasted image 20260101040435.png]]

For a spring or a wire with extension $x$ and force $F$, the work done on the spring is equal to the elastic potential energy $E$.

$$
E = \text{area of triangle} 
$$
Thus:
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}Fx
$$
Which simplifies to:
$$
E = \frac{1}{2}(kx)x = \frac{1}{2}kx^{2}
$$

Thus for a given spring, $E \propto x^{2}$; doubling the extension will quadruple the energy stored by spring.
