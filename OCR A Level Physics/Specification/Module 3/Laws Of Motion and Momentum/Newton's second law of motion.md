$F=ma$ is a special case of Newton's second law of motion. It only occurs when the mass of an object is constant. 

The law relates the rate of change of momentum of an object to the resultant force acting on the object.

The resultant force acting on an object is directly proportional to the rate of change of its momentum and is in the same direction, 

$$
\text{Resultant force} \propto \text{rate of change of momentum}
$$
Thus:
$$
\text{Resultant force} = k \times \text{rate of change of momentum}
$$
In SI units, the constant k is taken as equal to 1. This law can be rewritten as:
$$
F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t}
$$
## $F=ma$
For an object will constant mass $m$ with initial velocity $u$ and final velocity $v$, after a time $t$, the resultant force $F$ is given by:
$$
F = \frac{\Delta p}{\Delta t} = \frac{mv-mu}{t} = m\left( \frac{v-u}{t} \right) = ma
$$
