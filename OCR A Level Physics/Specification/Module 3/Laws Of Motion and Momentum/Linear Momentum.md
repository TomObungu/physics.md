The linear momentum is defined as the product of the mass $m$ of an object and its velocity $v$:
$$
p = mv
$$
The SI unit for momentum is $kgms ^{-1}$

# Conservation of momentum
The total momentum in a specified direction remains constant. As long as no external forces act on the system.

In a closed system, where no external forces act, the total initial momentum is equal to the total final momentum.

- In all collisions, the total energy and the total momentum remain constant
- In perfectly elastic collisions the total kinetic energy remain constant
- In an inelastic collision, there is a change in kinetic energy into another form.

# Worked Example: Explosive
![[Pasted image 20260101082257.png]]
Before the explosion, the initial momentum is zero. Therefore the final momentum must also be zero. 
$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{Final momentum} = \text{Initial Momentum} \\ \\
\text{Let } m = \text{total mass of rock } \\ \\ 
0.7(m)(15) + 0.3(m)v = 0 \\ \\ 
v = -\frac{0.7(m)(15)}{0.3(m)} =  -35ms^{-1}
\end{gather*}
$$
The minus signifies the fragments move in opposite directions. The speed of the smaller fragment is $35ms^{-1}$.
$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{ratio} = \frac{\frac{1}{2}(0.3)(35)^{2}}{\frac{1}{2}(0.7)(15)^{2}} = 2.3
\end{gather*}
$$
