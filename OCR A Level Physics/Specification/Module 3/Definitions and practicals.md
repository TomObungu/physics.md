# 3. 1 Motion

- Acceleration - the rate of change of velocity

- Velocity - The rate of change of displacement

- Average velocity - Displacement / time taken

- Reaction time is the time taken to process a stimulus and trigger a response to it. It is affected by alcohol, drugs and tiredness. 

- Thinking distance - the distance travelled between the moment when you first see a reason to stop to the moment when you use the brake

- Braking distance - The distance travelled from the time the brake is applied until the vehicle stops

# 3.2  Forces in Action

- Archimedes' principle - the upthrust exerted on a body immersed in a fluid, whether fully or partially submerged, is equal to the weight of the fluid that body displaces.

- Centre Of Gravity - The point in where the entire weight of the object appears to act

- Centre of mass - The point through which any externally applied force produces straight-line motion. 

- Pressure is the **normal force** exerted on a surface per unit cross-sectional area:

- The principle of moments sates that the sum of the clockwise moments about a point or an axis equal to the sum of the anticlockwise moments about the same point or axis.

- Equilibrium - both the resultant force and resultant moment acting on the object must be equal to zero.

- The atmospheric pressure at sea level is $101kPa$. 

- The density of water is about $1.03 \times 10^{3}kgm^{-3}$.

- A couple is two equal and opposite parallel forces that act in an anti parallel direction to each other. Each force is the same distance from the pivot. It has the effect of causing a rotation without translation.

- Torque of a couple is defined as the product of one of the forces and the perpendicular separation between the forces.

- The Newton is defined as the resultant force that will give a mass of $1kg$ acceleration of $1ms ^{-2}$

- Moment is the product of the force applied and the perpendicular distance of the line action of the force from the pivot.

- The terminal velocity is the maximum velocity of an object that occurs when the resistive and driving forces acting on the object are equal to each other.

# 3.3 Work, Energy and Power

- The principle of conservation of energy states that the total energy of a closed system remains constant - energy can never be created or destroyed, but it can be transferred from one to another.

- Conservation of energy - the total energy of a closed system remains constant - energy can never be created or destroyed, but it can be transferred from one to another. 

- Work done  - the product of force $F$ and the distance $x$ moved in the direction of the force

- Gravitational potential energy is the energy gained by an object when it is raised by a height in a gravitational field

-  Newtons first law - object will remain at rest or continue to move with constant velocity unless acting upon by a resultant force

- Newton's third law - when two objects interact they exert equal and opposite forces on each other.

- Newton's second - the resultant force acting on an object is directly proportional to the rate of change of its momentum and is in the same direction

# 3.4 Materials

- Brittle  - a material that  shows elastic behaviour up to its breaking point, without any plastic deformation. 

 - Ductile - a material that can easily be drawn into a wire or hammered into thin sheets.

- Elastic deformation - The material will return to its original shape when the deforming forces are removed. 

- Hooke's Law - The extension of an elastic object will be directly proportional to the force applied to it up to the object’s limit of proportionality.
