# Kinetic energy

## Deriving $E_{k} = \frac{1}{2}mv^{2}$
For an object initially at rest. The acceleration of the object is $a$. After a distance $s$, it has a speed $v$.
![[Pasted image 20260101033923.png]]

The distance $s$ travelled by the object can be determined by the equation:
$$
\begin{gather*}
v^{2} = u^{2}+2as \\ \\ 
s = \frac{v^{2}-u^{2}}{2a} = \frac{v^{2}}{2a} (u =0) \\ \\
\end{gather*}
$$
The work done is given by:
$$
 W = E_{k} = Fs 
$$
Therefore
$$
\begin{gather*}
E_{k} = ma \times s  \\ \\
E_{k} = \frac{1}{2}mv^{2}
\end{gather*}
$$
For an object with initial speed $u$ and final speed $v$, the change in kinetic energy is:
$$
\frac{1}{2}m(v^{2}-u^{2})
$$
# Gravitational potential energy (near Earth's surface)
$$
E_{p} = mgh
$$

According to the principle of conservation of energy, the sum of the $KE$ and $GPE$ of the object at any instant must be constant, as long as there are no thermal losses:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{Change in GPE} = \text{Change in KE} \\ \\
mgh = \frac{1}{2}mv^{2} \\ \\
v^{2} = 2gh \\ 
v = \sqrt{ 2gh }
\end{gather*}
$$
