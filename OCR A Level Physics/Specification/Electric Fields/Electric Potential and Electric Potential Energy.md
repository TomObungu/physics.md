Consider two positive charges being pushed closer together. The charges will repel each other, thus more work has to be done to decrease the separation between the charges and the separation decreases, the force need increases.

![[Pasted image 20251211005527.png]]

In this case, decreasing the separation between the changes results in a quadratic increase for force required as $F=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}}$ thus $F\propto \frac{1}{r^{2}}$ 


# Force-distance graph for point and spherical charges
For a uniformly charged sphere, it can be treated a point and be modelled with $F=\frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}}$, where $r$ also considers the distance from the centre of the sphere. 

For a graph of $F$ against separation $r$, the area under the graph is equal to the work done. This is from definition of work done being force multiplied by distance ($W=Fd$)

![[Pasted image 20251211010524.png]]

Much like gravitational potential, the total work done to bring the particles from infinity to a separation $r$ is the total area under the graph in the above diagram.
