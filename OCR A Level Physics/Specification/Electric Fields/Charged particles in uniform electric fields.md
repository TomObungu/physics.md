# Acceleration
An electron between the plates will travel away from the negative plate towards the positive plate, in the opposite direction of the electric field. The electron experiences a constant electrostatic force because of the uniform field. 

- The electric field strength $E$ between the plates is given by $E=\frac{V}{d}$, where $V$ is the p.d between the plates and $d$ as the separation between the plates

- Force $F$ on the electron is given by $F=EQ=Ee$, where $e$ is the elementary charge

- Work done on the electron by the electric field $W=Vq=Ve$

## Worked Example
![[Pasted image 20251211002904.png]]
You are given $V$, $d$,$m$, $e$ and $v$ and you want to calculate $s$. 

We know that $E=\frac{V}{d}$, therefore 
we calculate the force $F$:
$$
F=EQ=Ee = \frac{600}{3 \times 10^{-2}}(1.60\times 10^{-19}) = 3.2 \times 10 ^{-15}
$$
Calculate the acceleration $a$ using $F=ma$:
$$
F=ma\implies a=\frac{F}{m} = \frac{3.2 \times 10^{-15}}{9.11 \times 10^{-31}} = 3.513 \times 10^{15}
$$
Use $v^{2}=u^{2}+2as$ to calculate $s$ when the electron stops at $v=0$:
$$
v^{2}=u^{2}+2as\implies s=-\frac{u^{2}}{2a} = -\frac{(1 \times 10^{7})^{2}}{2(3.513\times 10^{15})} = 1.4 \times 10^{-2}
$$

## Charged particles moving at right angles to an electric field
Objects on the surface of the Earth describe a parabolic path - Only the vertical motion of is affected by the Earth's gravitational pull whereas the horizontal component is constant. 

The same applies to charged particles when they enter a uniform electric field.

![[Pasted image 20251211003728.png]]

You can predict the motion of the particle in the electric field by analysing its vertical and horizontal components of motion independently, as if you analysing a projectile in kinematic motion on the surface of the Earth.

### Horizontal Motion of particle
- There is no acceleration, hence the horizontal velocity $V_{H}$ of the particle remains constant, with velocity $v$
- The time spent is equal $t=\frac{L}{v}$ where $L$ is the horizontal length the electron travels in the uniform electric field

## Vertical Motion of particle
- The vertical acceleration $a$ of the particle is given by the equation:
$$
a = \frac{F}{m} = \frac{EQ}{m}
$$
- The initial vertical velocity of the particle is $u$, with $u=0$
- The final vertical component of the charged particle $V_{V}$ as the particle exists the field is given by the equation:
$$
V_{V} = u+at=0+\frac{EQ}{m}t=0+\frac{EQ}{m}\left( \frac{L}{v} \right)= \frac{EQL}{mv} 
$$
