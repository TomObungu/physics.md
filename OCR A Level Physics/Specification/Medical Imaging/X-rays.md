X-rays were discovered in 1895 by Wilhem Rontgen.  This was after applying p.d between electrodes inside a gas tube. 
# Nature of X-rays
Experiments show that x-rays can be polarised, diffracted by atoms in crystals and have extremely short wavelengths of $10^{-8}$ to $10^{-13}$. They are electromagnetic waves so they can travel at the speed of light in a vacuum. 

X-ray photons have $10^{4}$ times more energy than a photon of visible light. X-rays are harmful to living cells and can kill them. This property is what is used to treat cancer. 

# Productions of X-rays
X-rays are produced when fast-moving electrons are decelerated by interaction with atoms of a metal such as tungsten. The kinetic energy of the electrons is transformed into photons with the x-ray frequency.  

A radiography  machine contains an X-ray tube that produces X-ray photons that pass through the patient to the detection plate below it. Digital detection plates have replaced photographic plates to allow the storage of x-ray photons, media of images share through computers and enhancement of image to detect any changes in tissues or bones.

X-ray tubes consist of evacuated tubes containing two electrodes. Evacuated means that the electrons mass through the tube without interacting with gas atoms. An external power supply creates a large p.d of 30-100 kV between the electrodes. 

The cathode is a heater and produces electrons through thermionic emission. These electrons are accelerated towards the anode. The anode is made from a metal, known as the target metal, such as tungsten, that has a high melting point. 

X-ray photons are produces when electrons are decelerated by hitting the anode. The energy output of X-rays is less than 1% of the kinetic energy of the incident electrons. The remainder is transformed into thermal energy of the anode. Many X-ray tubes utilise oil circulation to cool the tube or rotation of the anode to spread heat over a large surface area.

The anode is shaped such that the X-rays are emitted in the desired direction through a window. The X-ray tube is lined with lead to shield the radiographer from any X-rays emitted in other directions.

# The shortest wavelength
An electron accelerated through a p.d $V$ gains kinetic energy of $eV$. Since one electron releases one X-ray photon, from the principle of conservation of energy, the maximum energy of the photon must also be the maximum kinetic energy of a single electron. 

$$
\text{maximum energy of photon } = \text{ maxmimum kinetic energy of electron}
$$

The energy of a photon is equal to the Planck constant, $h$, multiplied by frequency, $f$. The maximum frequency of the x-ray, $f$, is the speed $c$ divided by wavelength, $\lambda$, so:
$$
\begin{gather*}
hf = eV  \\ \\
\frac{hc}{\lambda} = eV \\ \\
\therefore \lambda = \frac{hc}{eV}
\end{gather*}
$$
The wavelength from an x-ray tube is inversely proportional to the accelerating potential difference. Increasing the tube current just increase then intensity of the  x-rays.
