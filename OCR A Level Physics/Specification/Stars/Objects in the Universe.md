# Star birth
## Nebulae
Nebulae are astronomical clouds of dust and gas (mainly hydrogen).  They are formed over millions of years as the tiny gravitational attraction between the particles pulls the particles towards each other. As the dust gets closer, the gravitational collapse accelerates. Variations within the nebula cause some regions to be denser. In these dense regions, more dust and gas is pulled into the region and then region becomes more massive, denser and hotter as gravitational energy is transformed into thermal energy. 

## Protostar
Eventually, in one part of the cloud, a protostar forms - a very hot and dense sphere of dust and gas. 

For a protostar to become a star, nuclear fusion needs to start in its core. 

Extreme pressures and temperatures are required to overcome the electrostatic repulse between the protons in hydrogen nuclei in order to fuse them together to form helium nuclei. 

In some cases, more and more mass is added to the protostar.

# Star life
At around $10^7K$, nuclear fusion begins and a star is born. Hydrogen nuclei are fused together to make helium nuclear as nuclear fusion begins. At this point mass is converted into energy

Once it is formed, it remains in stable equilibrium with almost a constant size. 

Gravitational forces acts to compress the star, but the radiation pressure from the photons emitted and gas pressure from nuclei in core  during nuclear fusion push outwards:
$$
\text{Gravitation} = \text{Radiation Pressure}
$$

The force from the radiation and gas pressure balance the force from gravitational attraction and maintains equilibrium.

Stars in this phase are described as main sequence stars. The duration of being in main sequence depends on the size and mass of its core. 

Supergiant stars are much hotter than small stars like our sun. They release more power and convert the available hydrogen into helium in a much shorter time. These stars are only stable for a few million year.

Smaller stars like our Sun are stable for tens of billions of years.

### Our sun
Our sun is currently in a main sequence star and has been estimated to have lived 4.5 billions in main sequence. Our sun is estimated to live for another 5 billions years before collapsing into a red giant
