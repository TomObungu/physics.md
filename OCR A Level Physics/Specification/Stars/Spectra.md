It is possible to analyse light from stars to determine specific elements by comparing known elements with the wavelength of the photons being emitted. 

The technique of analysing the light from stars is the science of spectroscopy. Spectroscopy is possible as atoms have different spectral lines. The spectra from starlight can be used identify the elements with stars, without a direct sample from the star.

# Continuous, emission, and absorption spectra

## Continuous line
All visible frequencies or wavelengths are present. The atoms of a heated solid metal will produce this type of spectrum.

In terms of differentiating the type of metal, the intensities of the the specific frequencies can be differentiated for the different metals.

## Emission line
Each element produces a unique emission line spectrum because of its unique set of energy levels.  

If the atoms in a gas are excited, e.g. within the extreme environments of a stars, when they are de-excited, they emit photons with a set of discrete frequencies specific to that element. 

This produces a characteristic emission line spectrum. Each spectral line corresponds to photons with a specific wavelength. These spectra can be observed in a laboratory from heated gases.

## Absorption line 
An absorption line spectrum is formed when a light from a source that produces a continuous spectrum passes through a cooler gas. As the photons pass through the gas, some photons are absorbed by the gas atoms, raising electrons up into higher energy level and so exciting the atoms of the gas.  

Only photons with energy exactly equal to the difference between the energy levels of the light is absorbed by the gas. This means that only specific wavelengths are absorbed, which creates dark lines in the spectrum.

### Why
The photons absorbed by the gas are re-emitted when the electron drops back down to a lower energy level atom, they are emitted in all possible directions, so the intensity in the original direction is greatly reduced.
