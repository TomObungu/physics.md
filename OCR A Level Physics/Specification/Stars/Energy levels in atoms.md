Neon lights do not have a hot filament, only neon gas or another gas at low pressure. When a large enough p.d is supplied across the tube, the gas glows, giving of a characteristic colour depending on the gas. The properties of the light emitted are dependant on the behaviour of the electrons with in its atoms.

# Energy levels in gas atoms
When electrons are bound to their atoms in a gas they can only exist in one of few a discrete set of quantum mechanical states due to the Pauli exclusion principle. If the set of quantum mechanical states are also discrete this also means that specific energy levels are also discrete. 

- All of the quantum mechanical states or energy states are discrete - they cannot have an energy state between two different energy states
- The energy levels are negative in the representation of the energy levels are external energy will be required to remove an electron from an atom. In other words if an electron has an energy state of -6.8eV, it will take 6.8eV to free the electron from the atom. 
	- Therefore an electron with an energy state of zero is free from the atom
- The energy level with the most negative value is known as the ground level or the ground state

When an electron moves from a lower to higher energy level within an atom or a gas, the atom is said to be excited. Raising an electron into higher energy level requires external energy. An example of this external energy could be:
- Supplying an electric field with an accelerating p.d -> $E=hf$
- Heating the gas -> $E_{k} = \frac{3}{2}kT$
- If the gas absorbs photons of a specific frequency -> $E=hf$)
