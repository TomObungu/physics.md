The gravitational potential at a point in a gravitational field is defined as the work done per unit mass to move an object to that point from infinity. 

## Why use Gravitational Potential
https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2_bDJmfdIPA
Much like electric potential i.e Voltage, gravitational potential is the work done per unit mass to move an object in a gravitational whereas voltage is the work required to move an object in an electric field.  

It is useful as the value of the strength of the gravitational field varies in larger scales in where radial gravitational fields comes to play. 

Simply using $GPE=mgh$ is not credible as it only works on the surface of the Earth where the gravitational field is considered as a uniform gravitational and changes in height yield negligible effects on the value of $g=9.81$. 

In other words $GPE=mgh$ is only an approximation that only valid near the Earth's surface. It relies on the assumptions:
	- The gravitational field is inform which means the value of $g$ is relatively constant
	- The change is height $h$ is small enough that the change in $g$ is negligible

However for much larger astronomical scales, $GPE=mgh$ begins to break down as the field strength $g$ follows the inverse-square law and changes significantly with distance due to $g=\frac{GM}{r^{2}}$.

Therefore in order to have a more reliable that astronomical scales for cases when the gravitational field is radial and for different planets other than our earth, we must use gravitational potential:

You can see that the gravitational potential is account for masses of planets other an our Earth where $M$ is different.
$$
V_{g} = -\frac{GM}{r}
$$
where $M$ is the point mass of the body producing the gravitational field.

You can see that is the simply the work divided by the secondary mass within the gravitational field:
$$
V_{g} = \frac{W}{m}
$$
This is beneficial as in order to calculate the GPE again we simply need to multiply the secondary mass that is being moved in the gravitational field.

This is where the usefulness of gravitational potential comes in as we can now account for the different values of $g$ when dealing with gravitational fields that are not our Earth's or are much further away from the surface of the Earth.

The unit is $Jkg^{-1}$. Infinity refers to the distance. This is not to be confused with gravitational potential energy where the height gained from the object in the gravitational field will increase the potential for work to be done by the field. 

The gravitational force is always attractive and at infinity it is always zero.

## Gravitational Potential in a radial field
In a radial field, the field depends on:
- The distance r from the point mass producing the gravitational field
- The mass $M$ of the point mass
![[Pasted image 20251022083718.png]]

$Vg \propto M$ and $Vg\propto \frac{1}{r}$

Therefore the $V_{g}$ is given by:
$$
V_{g} = -\frac{GM}{r}
$$
The units are $Jkg^{-1 }$
All values of $V_{g}$ will be negative for $0<r< \infty$

## Changes in $V_{g}$
- Moving towards the point mass $M$ will result in a decrease in gravitational potential
- Moving away from the point mass $M$ will result in an increase in gravitational potential
