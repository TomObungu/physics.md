Using Kepler's law of planetary motion, we can model satellites orbiting the Earth. The centripetal force on each satellite is provided between the gravitational force between it and the Earth. 

For any satellite, $M$ is the mass of the Earth, $v$ is the constant speed of the satellite, $G$ is the gravitational constant and $r$ is the distance from the satellite to the centre of the Earth.
$$
F = \frac{mv^{2}}{r}=\frac{GMm}{r^{2}}
$$

## Why satellites remain at the same height above the surface
The only force acting on a satellite is the gravitational attraction between it and the Earth, it is always 'falling towards Earth'. However as it is travelling so fast, it travels such a great distance that as it falls, the Earth curves away beneath it, keeping at the same height above the surface.

For any given satellite, the correct speed $v$ for a stable orbit at a distance $r$, from the centre of mass of the Earth is given by:
$$
v = \sqrt{ \frac{GM}{r} }
$$

### Geostationary satellites
A geostationary satellite is placed in specific orbit so it remains above the same point of Earth whilst the Earth rotates. In order to do this:
- Be in orbit above the Earth's equator
- Rotate in the same direction as the Earth's rotation
- Have an orbital period of 24 hours.
