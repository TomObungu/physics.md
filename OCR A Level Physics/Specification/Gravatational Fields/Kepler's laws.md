## Kepler's first law
The orbit of a planet is an ellipse with the Sun at one of the two foci

An ellipse is a elongated circle. With two foci, the orbits of all the planets are elliptical. 

In most cases, the orbits have a low eccentricity ( a measure of how elongated a circle is), and so their orbits are modelled as circles 

## Kepler's second law
A line segment joining a planet and the Sun sweep out equal area during intervals of time.

This is because due to the elliptical nature of the planets around the sun, the planet's speed is not constant. When a planet gets closer to the sun in orbit, the radial distance from the sun decreases but as a result the planet's speed increases. # Kepler's third law
The square of orbital period $T$ of a planet is directly proportional to the cube of its average distance $r$ from the Sun.

This can be written as a relationship as $T^{2}\propto r^{3}$ or mathematically:
$\frac{T^{2}}{r^{3}}=k$. 

The mean distance between the Earth and the Sun is known as one astronomical unit.

### Modelling planetary orbits as circles

$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{mv^{2}}{r} = \frac{GMm}{r^{2}} \\ \\
v^{2} = \frac{GM}{r}
\end{gather*}
$$
