# Pressure and Volumes
If the temperature and mass of a gas remain constant, then the pressure $p$ of an ideal gas is inversely proportional to its volume $V$. This can be expressed as:
$$
p\propto\frac{1}{V}
$$ or:
$$
\begin{gather*}
p = \frac{k}{V} \\ \
pV=k
\end{gather*}
$$
This means that:
$$
pV = \text{constant}
$$
If a fixed mass of a gas is kept in a sealed box, halving the volume of the box (slowly, to ensure the temperature remains constant) will compress the gas and double the pressure it exerts on the box.

This is Boyle's Law.

# Pressure and temperature
If the volume and mass of gas remain constant, the pressure $p$ of an ideal gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature $T$ in kelvin. This relationship can be expressed as:
$$
p\propto T
$$
or as:
$$
\begin{gather*}
p = kT \\ \\
\frac{p}{T} = k
\end{gather*}
$$
which can be rewritten as:
$$
\frac{p}{T} = \text{constant}
$$
For a fixed mass of gas sealed in a container, doubling the temperature will double pressure.
