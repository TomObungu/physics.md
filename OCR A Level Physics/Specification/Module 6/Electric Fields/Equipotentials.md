An equipotential line is a line or a surface along which if a particle were to follow the trajectory of the line, the the magnitude of electric potential will remain the same. 

![[Pasted image 20251211041348.png]]

In the image above, if a test charge Q where to travel across the green line, the magnitude of electric potential will remain the same, despite the particle changing its position.  

Equipotential lines indicate the the change in electric potential with respect to the distance from the charge $r$, which is the electric potential gradient. Thus the magnitude of an equipotential line shows:
$$
\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta r}
$$

If a test charge $Q$ is moved through a uniform field, the change in electric potential energy is $W=-Q\Delta V$. This energy change is equal to work done or energy gained from electric potential energy. 

From, the definition of work done, work done is equal to the force multiplied by the distance travelled $F\Delta r$ . 

Note that electric potential relates to the energy gained by the test charge $Q$, however,

The force the accelerates the test charge $Q$ through the field is relating to the electric field strength which is:
$$
F = EQ
$$
Thus we know that:
$$
W = Fd = EQ\Delta r
$$
also,

Thus equating $W$ gives:
$$
-Q\Delta V=EQ\Delta r
$$
Therefore:
$$
\begin{gather*}
-\cancel{ Q }\Delta V=E\cancel{ Q }\Delta r \\ \\
E = -\frac{\Delta V}{\Delta r}
\end{gather*}
$$
Thus the electric field strength will be equal to magnitude of the equipotential line multiplied by $-1$
