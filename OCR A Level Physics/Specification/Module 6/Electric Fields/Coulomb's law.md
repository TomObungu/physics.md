The French physicist, Charles Augustin Coulomb (1736-1806) formulated a law analogous to Newton's law of gravitation but for charged particles. Coulomb's law states that any two point charges are exert an electrostatic force on each other that is directly proportional to their charges and inversely proportional to the square of their distances.  

For two like point charges and two unline point charges with a separation $r$. The magnitudes of the charges are $Q$ and $q$. The electrostatic force experienced by each point charge is $F$.
![[Pasted image 20251204174729.png]]
From that statement of Coulomb's law:
$$
F \propto Qq
$$
and:
$$
F \propto \frac{1}{r^{2}}
$$

Thus we can write the equation as:
$$
F = k \frac{Qq}{r^{2}}
$$
And we can also deduce that:
$$
\begin{gather*}
\frac{F}{\frac{Qq}{r^{2}}} = k \\ \\ 
\frac{Fr^{2}}{Qq} = k
\end{gather*}
$$
Therefore:
$$
\frac{F_{1}r_{1}^{2}}{Q_{1}q_{1}} = \frac{F_{2}r_{2}^{2}}{Q_{2}q_{2}}
$$


The value of $k$ is the permittivity of free space $\varepsilon_{0}$.  $\varepsilon_{0}$ is equal to :
$$
 k =\frac{1}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}} = 8.85 \times 10 ^{-12}
$$
Thus writing the full expression for electrostatic force that two charged molecules exert on each other as they vary with distance can be written as:
$$
F = \frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}}
$$

## Investigating Coulomb's law
It is possible to apply Coulomb's law to large uniformly charges spheres too. The value of $r$ will be separation between the centre of the two spheres

Below is a technique for investigating the forces between two charged metal spheres.  Using table tennis balls coated in conductive paint. The balls can be charged positively by touching each momentarily to the positive electrode of a high tension supply. 

Lowering one of the charged spheres down towards the other will produce a larger reading on the balance.

## Worked Example
![[Pasted image 20251204175943.png]]
Firstly calculate the minimum value of  $r$. The minimum value of $r$ will occur when the spheres are almost touching but no quite. This means the separation $r$ will be $r_{1} +r_{2}$ which is $(1.8+2.5)=4.3 \times 10 ^{-2}$. 

After use Coulomb's law to calculate the force:
$$
F = \frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}} = \frac{(4 \times 10 ^{-9})(-5.2 \times 10 ^{-9})}{4\pi(8.85 \times 10^{-12})(4.3 \times 10 ^{-2})} = -1.0 \times 10 ^{-4}
$$

It is possible to calculate the electric field strength $E$ of a point charge $Q$ as the force $F$ experienced by a positive test charge divide by the charge $q$ on the test charge. Therefore:
$$
E = \frac{F}{q} = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}q} = \frac{Q}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}}
$$
![[Pasted image 20251204180529.png]]
The electric field strength is thus directly proportional to the charge $Q$ and obeys an inverse square law relationship with distance $r$. A graph of $\frac{1}{r^{2}}$ will be a straight line through the origin.

## Differences between gravitational and electric fields
The equation for gravitational and electric fields are very similar however the main difference is that gravitational fields are always attractive whereas charges can create both attractive and repulsive fields.
