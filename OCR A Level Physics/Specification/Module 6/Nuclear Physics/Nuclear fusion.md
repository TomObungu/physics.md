The Sun generates its energy by fusion, a process by which small nuclei are combined to make large nuclei. 

# Fusion reactions
Nuclei fusion within a few femtometers of each other. This allows the short range strong nuclear force to attract them into a larger nucleus. 

All nuclei have positive charge and so will repel each other. This repulsive force is large and small separations. At low temperatures, the nuclei do not have enough kinetic energy to trigger fusion, however higher temperatures allow absorption through the strong nuclear force. 

The conditions for fusion are temperatures close to $1.4 \times 10^{7}K$ and density of nuclei $1.5 \times 10^{5} kgm^{-3}$. 

# Examples of fusion
These are examples of fusion reactions taking place within fusion environments such as stars. Fusion reactions often occur in cycles or sequences. 

- Two protons fuse together to produce a deuterium nucleus $(^{2}_{1}H)$, a positron and a neutrino:
$$
^{1}_{1}p + ^{1}_{1}p \to ^{2}_{1}H + ^{0}_{+1}e + v
$$
	- The two single protons have zero binding energy and the deuterium nucleus has a binding energy of $1.1 \times 2$ = 2.2 Mev. The difference in the binding energies. This difference in binding energies is the energy released in this fusion reaction.
- The deuterium nucleus from the first reaction fuses with a proton. A helium-3 nucleus is formed. 
- The helium-3 from the second reaction combines with another helium-3 nucleus. A helium-4 nucleus is formed, together with two protons.
This whole cycle is repeated again with two protons. This cycle is known as the proton-proton cycle or the hydrogen burning cycle.
