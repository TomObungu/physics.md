When an electron is moving towards, the negative plate of a capacitor, the electron will experience a repulsive electrostatic force from all electrons on the plate. In order to overcome this repulsive force, external work has to be done to push the electron. This external work is done by the battery. ![[Pasted image 20251126012900.png]]
## p.d against charge graphs
The under a  graph of p.d against charge will be work done. The small amount of work done by the battery or supply to increase the charge of the charge of capacitor by a small amount $\Delta Q$ is:
$$
\Delta W \approx V\Delta Q
$$
If you were take this work done in consideration to a p.d-charge graph, this area will be a small rectangular strip. If you were to add all the similar strips together, then the area under this graph is the total work done on the charges for a charging capacitor.
## Stored energy
The work done on the charges is the same as the energy stored in the capacitor. This energy is potential and can be release again. Since the area underneath is equal to the work done. An equation for the potential energy stored in a capacitor can be derived.
![[Pasted image 20251126013503.png]]
The graph of p.d against charge is a straight line graph due to $Q=VC$ thus $V\propto Q$. This means the area of the graph can be found using the equation of a triangle:
$$
W = A = \text{Area of triangle } = \frac{1}{2}QV
$$
Substituting $Q=VC$ into the equation gives:
$$
W = \frac{1}{2}(VC)V
$$
And thus:
$$
W = \frac{1}{2}CV^{2}
$$
This forms a quadratic relationship between $V$, $W\propto V^{2}$ and the energy done the charge carriers in the capacitor. $W$
