# Hadrons and quarks
Any particle that contains quarks is called a hadrons (as well as being affected by the strong nuclear force). It takes a small number of quarks and anti-quarks to make up the hundreds of hadrons discovered in collisions in particle accelerators. 

# Standard model of elementary particles
All quarks have a charge $Q$ that is fraction of the elementary charge $e$. 
Below is a summary of the relevant quarks required for this course. 

| Name    | Symbol | Charge          | Anti-quark Name | Anti-quark symbol | Charge          |
| ------- | ------ | --------------- | --------------- | ----------------- | --------------- |
| up      | u      | $+ \frac{2}{3}$ | anti-up         | $\bar{u}$         | $- \frac{2}{3}$ |
| down    | d      | $- \frac{1}{3}$ | anti-down       | $\bar{d}$         | $+\frac{1}{3}$  |
| strange | s      | $- \frac{1}{3}$ | anti-strange    | $\bar{s}$         | $+\frac{1}{3}$  |

# Protons and neutrons
The individual quarks inside Hadrons are bound together by the strong nuclear force. This force is believed to be strong enough to prevent separation of individual quarks. 

A proton consists of three quarks - up, up and down or better, $u,u,d$

Even at this subatomic level, the principle of conservation is upheld here. Thus the charge of a proton can be calculated as:
$$
\text{proton charge } Q = \left( +\frac{2}{3} + \frac{2}{3}-\frac{1}{3} \right)e = +1e
$$

A neutron consists of three quarks, but this time they are up, down, down or better $u,d,d$.

# Mesons and baryons
Baryons are any hadrons made with a combination of three quarks. These include protons, neutrons and antiprotons. In an antiprotons, the combination is $\bar{u},\bar{u},\bar{d}$, this works as the combination is specifically three. 

Mesons are hadrons made with a quark and anti-quark pair.
