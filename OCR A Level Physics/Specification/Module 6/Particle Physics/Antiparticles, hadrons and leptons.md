# Antimatter
In 1928, Paul Dirac's theory predicted every particle has a corresponding antiparticle. If two antiparticles meet, they completely destroy each other in a process called annihilation. The mass of the particles is then converted into high-energy pair of photons. 

Antiparticles have the opposite charge to the particle if the particle has charge. Antiparticles also have the exact same rest mass as the particle.

The antiparticle of the electron is the positron. A positrons mass is the same as an electron at $9.11 \times 10^{-31}kg$ but charge $-e$($-1.60 \times 10^{-19}$). There exists the antiproton, antineutron and the antineutrino. Antiparticles are symbolised by a bar over the letter for the particle. E.g The letter for a neutron is $v$, thus an antiparticle is denoted as $\bar{v}$.

# Fundamental particles 
A fundamental particle has no internal structure and hence cannot be divided into smallebits. 

## Hadrons
Hadrons are particles and antiparticles that are affected by the strong nuclear force. These include, protons, neutrons and mesons. Charged Hadrons aslo experience electromagnetic force. Hadrons decay by the weak nuclear force

## Leptons
Leptons are particles and antiparticles that are not affected by the strong nuclear force. These include, electrons, neutrinos and muons. Leptons if charge also experience electromagnetic forces.
