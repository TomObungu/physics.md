Neutrinos are leptons and are less than a millionth the mass of an electrons.  The existence of the neutron was predicted by Wolfgang Pauli in 1930 in order to explain beta decay in terms of conservation laws.

The three types of neutrinos are:
- Electron neutrino $v_{e}$
- Muon neutrino $v_{\mu}$
- Tau neutrino $v_{\tau}$]

Each neutrino has its antiparticle. For OCR A level physics, the only relevant neutrino is the electron neutron $v_{e}$ and its corresponding antiparticle $\bar{v}_{e}$

# Beta decay
Unstable nuclei emit radiation in various forms. Alpha $\alpha$ - emits a helium nuclei (alpha particle), $\beta$, - emits an electron ($\beta^{-}$) or positron $\beta^{+}$ and gamma radiation is the emission of high energy gamma photons. 

These emissions are to do with changes taking place within the nuclei of the atoms. For beta decay, the changes occur to the neutrons or protons. As stated previously, the force responsible for beta decay is the weak nuclear force.

In $\beta^{-}$ decay,  a neutron in an unstable nucleus decays into a proton, electron, and electron antineutrino.

# Quark transformation
Each type of beta decay is associated with the decay of a specific quark within the proton or the neutron. 
![[Pasted image 20260130080743.png]]

In $\beta^{-}$, the down quark becomes an up quark and in the process an electron and electron antineutrino are emitted - The down quark decays into an up quark and releases this neutrinos/antineutrino pair.

As you can see, the charge of the left-hand side is $-\frac{1}{3}e$, and the total charge on the right hand side is $\frac{2}{3}e$.  

Similarly, in $\beta^{+}$ decay, one of the up quarks become a down quark, and in the process a positron and electron neutrino and emitted.

The total charge on both sides of the quake is $+\frac{2}{3}e$ as expected.
