The nucleus of atoms contains positive protons and uncharged neutrons. The proton and the neutron have approximately the same mass. The term nucleon is used to refer to either a proton or a neutron. The proton has a charge of $+e$. A neutral atom has the same number of electrons and protons.

# Isotopes
The nucleus of an atom for a particular element is represented as:
$$
\begin{gather*}
A_{X} \\
Z  \
\end{gather*}
$$
where $X$ is the chemical symbol for the element. $A$ is the nucleon number (the total number of protons and neutrons) and $Z$ is the proton number (also known as atomic number). The number of neutrons $N$ in the nucleus is thus $N=(A-Z)$

Isotopes are nuclei of the same element that have the same number of protons but differing number of neutrons. All isotopes of an element undergo the same chemical reactions.

# Atomic mass units
The masses of atoms and nuclear particles are often expressed in atomic mass units (u). One atomic mass unit $(1u)$ is one twelfth the mass of a neutral carbon-12 atoms. The experimental value of $1u$ is about $1.661 \times^{-27}$.

# Nuclear size and density
The radius of the nucleus depends on the nucleon number $A$ of the nucleus. Fast-moving electrons have a de Brogile wavelength of about $10^{-15}m$. Diffraction of such electrons have been used to determine the radii of isotopes. Thus the radius $R$ of a nucleus is given by the equation:
$$
R = r_{0}A^{\frac{1}{3}}
$$
Where $r_{0}$ has a base value of about $1.2fm$ or $1.2 \times 10^{-15}$ and $A$ is nucleon number. 

The nucleus of an atoms is very small, massive and hence extremely dense. A nuclei have a density of about $10^{17}$. 


# Nature of the strong nuclear force
In a helium-4 nucleus, the two protons are separated by a distance of about $10^{-15}m$ and exert a large repulsive electrostatic force on each other. According to  Coulomb's law, the repulsive electrostatic force $F$ is given by:
$$
\begin{gather*}
F = \frac{Qq}{4\pi\varepsilon_{0}r^{2}} \\ \\
= \frac{(1.60 \times 10^{-19})^{2}}{4\pi(8.85 \times 10^{-12})(10^{-15})^{2}} \approx 230N
\end{gather*}
$$
This is an extremely large repulsive force. Despite the attractive gravitational force between the protons being minuscule, about ($10^{-34}N$), there is another much stronger force acting on the protons. This force is the strong nuclear force.

## Worked Example 1
Estimate the density of a proton.

The radius of the proton can be calculated using $R=r_{0}A^{\frac{1}{3}}$. Since there is a single proton $A=1$ thus $R=r_{0}=1.2 \times 10^{-15}$. 

Using the mass of a proton being $1.7 \times 10^{-27}$ and the volume of a sphere formula being $V = \frac{4}{3}\pi R^{3}$. Thus:
$$
density = \frac{mass}{volume} = \frac{1.7 \times 10^{-27}}{\frac{4}{3}\pi(1.2 \times 10 ^{-15})^{3}} = 2 \times 10 ^{ 17}kgm^{-3}
$$
