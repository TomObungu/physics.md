It is possible to produce electrical currents use magnetism. Inside the generator of a large wind turbine, relative motion between a conductor and a magnetic field produces an electric current. 

# Investigating electromagnetic inductions
To induce an e.m.f  you need a coil and a magnet. A sensitive voltmeter attached to coil will initially show no reading when the coil and the magnetic are stationary. 

When the magnet is pushed towards the coil, an e.m.f is induced across the ends of the coil. Repeatedly pushing and pulling the magnet will induce an alternating current in the coil. The faster the magnet is moved, the larger the e.m.f induced.
![[Pasted image 20260119155851.png]]

There are other methods to induce e.m.f such a heavy weight and a d.c electric motor. The heavy weight falls to rotate the coil between the poles of the stationary magnet. 
![[Pasted image 20260119160205.png]]
An e.m.f is induced in a loop of copper wire when it is moved perpendicular to the magnetic field lines. The magnitude of the e.m.f is bigger when wire is pulled away faster from the magnetic field.

## Why?
Work done to move the magnet is transferred into electrical energy. The electrons in the coil experience a relative motion between the magnetic field. 

This relative motion causes the electrons to move due to experiencing a magnetic force given by $F=Bev$, where $B$ is the magnetic flux density, $e$ is the elementary charge and $v$ is the relative speed between the coil and the magnet. 

This movement of charged particles constitutes an electric current within the coil, so the process has produces electrical energy. 

# Magnetic flux & Magnetic flux linkage
Every experiment demonstrating electromagnetic induction can be explained in terms of magnetic flux $\phi$

The magnetic flux $\phi$ is defined as the product of the component of the magnetic flux density $B$ perpendicular to the area and the cross sectional area $A$. 
![[Pasted image 20260119160912.png]]

$$
\phi = BA\cos\theta
$$
where $\theta$ is the angle between the normal to the coil and the magnetic field. When the magnetic field is normal to the area, $\cos0=1$ and $\phi = BA$. The SI unit for magnetic flux is the weber $Wb$ and $1Wb = 1Tm^{2}$. 

Another quantity related to magnetic flux is called magnetic flux linkage. This is product of the number of turns in the coil $N$ and the magnetic flux:
$$
\text{magnetic flux linkage} = N\phi
$$
The SI unit for magnetic flux is also the weber, but sometimes weber-turns ($Wb-\text{turns}$  is used to avoid magnetic flux)

## e.m.f induction
An e.m.f is induced in a circuit whenever there is a **change** in the magnetic flux linking the circuit. Since $\phi = BA\cos\theta$, you can induce an e.m.f by constantly changing $B$, $A$ or $\theta$.
