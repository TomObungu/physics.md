The strength of magnets is measured in Tesla (T). The Earth's magnetic field is about $30\mu T$ at the Equator.

# Fleming's left hand rule

## Force on a current carrying conductor
A current carrying wire experiences a force when it is placed at right angles to the magnetic field of a magnet. According to Newton's third law, the wire experiences equal but opposite forces of the same type.

![[Pasted image 20260105090543.png]]

The direction of the force experience by the current-carrying wire in a uniform field can be determined using Fleming's left hand rule. 

The index finger points in the direction of the external field, the middle finger points in the direction of the conventional current and the thumb points in the direction of motion of the force
![[Pasted image 20260105090655.png]]
You can determine the direction of force by aligning the index and middle finger according to the field and current. The final position of the thumb determines the direction of the force. 

## Magnetic flux density
The force experienced $F$ experienced by the wire in a uniform magnetic field is given by the relationship:
$$
F \propto IL\sin\theta
$$
where $L$ is the length of the wire, $I$ is the current flowing through the wire and $\theta$ is the angle between the field and the wire.

The constant of proportionality for this equation is called the magnetic flux density $B$ of the magnetic field. Therefore:
$$
F = BIL\sin\theta
$$
The SI unit for flux density is Tesla and $1T = 1Nm ^{-1}A^{-1}$.  


The magnetic flux density is 1 Tesla when a wire carrying a current of $1A$ is placed perpendicular to the magnetic field and experiences a force of $1N$ per metre of its length 

When the wire is perpendicular to the magnetic field, $\theta=90$ and $\sin\theta=1$. Therefore:
$$
F = BIL
$$

## Determining magnetic flux density in the laboratory 
Magnets are placed on a top-pan balance. The magnetic field between them is almost uniform. 
![[Pasted image 20260105093000.png]]

A stiff copper wire is held perpendicular to the to the magnetic field between the two poles. 

The length $L$ of the wire in the magnetic field is measured using a metre rule. 

Using crocodile clips, a section of the copper wire is connected in series with an ammeter and a variable power supply. 

With current $I$, the wire experiences a vertical upward force as predicted by Fleming's left hand rule.  According to Newton's third law, the magnets on the top pan also experience an equal downward force. 

This force can be calculated by the change in mass reading $m$ and $F=mg$.

With force $F$, the magnetic flux density can be calculated using $B = \frac{F}{IL}$
