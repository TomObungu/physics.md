The counts and clicks from a GM tube do not show a regular pattern. The clicks are random and thus suggests the radioactive nuclei themselves must also decay in a random manner. Radioactive decay is described as a random and spontaneous event. 

It is random because:
- Cannot predict when a particular nucleus in a sample will decay or which one will decay next
- Each nucleus within a sample has the same chance of decaying per unit time.

It is spontaneous because decay of nucleus is not affected by:
- The presence of other nuclei in the sample
- External factors such as pressure

It is possible to simulate the behaviour of unstable nuclei flipping coins or rolling a large number of dice.

# Half life
A radioactive sample has trillions of nuclei and the probability of the decay of the nuclei depends on the isotope. 

The probability of decay governs who quickly the nuclei decay and therefore the half-life of the isotope.

**The half life of an isotope is the average time it takes for half the number of active nuclei in the sample to decay**

The mans that after at time equal to one half life $t$, the number $N$ of un-decayed nuclei in a sample will have halved. The number $N$ must therefore decay exponentially with time.

Unstable isotopes have half-lives ranging from fractions of a second to billions of years. 

# Activity - A
The count rate measured using a GM tube and counter is only a fraction of the rate at which particles, or photons are emitted by a radioactive source. The activity of the source will be much higher than the count rate. 

**The activity rate at which nuclei decay or disintegrate**

Think of activity rate as the number of alpha, beta or gamma photons emitted from the source per unit time. Activity is measured in decays per second. 

An activity rate of one decay per second is one becquerel $1Bq$. An activity rate of 2000$Bq$ from an alpha source means 2000 nuclei decay per second or 2000 alpha particles are emitted per second. The activity depends on the number of undecayed nuclei present in the source and on the half life of the the isotope. 

# Decay constant $\lambda$
Consider a source with a very large number of nuclei, with $N$ undecayed nuclei at $t=0$ that decay into a stable daughter nuclei. The decay is both random an spontaneous. In a small interval of time $\Delta t$, it would be reasonable to assume that the number of nuclei disintegrating would be directly proportional to $N$ and $\Delta t$, that is:
$$
\Delta N \propto N\Delta t
$$
Therefore:
$$
\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} \propto -N
$$

The minus shows that the number of nuclei is decreasing with time. $\frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t}$ is the rate of decay of the nuclei which is the activity $A$ of the soruce.

For a source containing a known isotope, the relationship may be written with a constant $\lambda$. This the decay constant of the isotope. Therefore:
$$
A = \frac{\Delta N}{\Delta t} = \lambda N
$$
The minus sign has now been omitted because we just need to know the value of activity. The decay constant has units $s ^{-1}.$

The decay constant can be defined as the probability of decay an individual nucleus per unit time.
