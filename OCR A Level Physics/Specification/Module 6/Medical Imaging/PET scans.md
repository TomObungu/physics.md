# Fluorine-18 
This versatile medical tracer is used in positron emission tomography. The isotope emits positrons and has half-life of 110 minutes. A nucleus of fluorine-18 decays into oxygen-18 in $\beta^{+}$ decay, emitting a gamma photon.
$$
^{18}_{9}F \to ^{18}_{8}O + ^{0}_{+1}e + v_{e} + \gamma 
$$
Fluorine is made by colliding high speed protons with oxygen-18. This collision produces fluorine-18 and free neutrons:
$$
^{1}_{1}p + ^{18}_{8}O \to ^{18}_{9}F + ^{1}_{0}n
$$

# Diagnosis using PET scans
PET scans use gamma radiation to construct detailed three-dimensional images. This is as opposed to X-ray radiation in CAT scanners. 

PET scanners use a medical traver called fluorodeoxyglucose (FDG). This trace is similar to naturally occurring glucose, however it is tagged with a radioactive fluorine-18 atom in place of an oxygen atom. FDG is beneficially as the human treats it as normal glucose. Once FDG is in the body, it accumulates in tissues with a high rate of respiration. The activity from the FDG is monitored using gamma detectors. 

Another medical tracer is carbon monoxide made with carbon-11. This isotope emits a positron and has half life of 20 minutes. Carbon monoxide is good at binding to haemoglobin. This allows it to be transported through the body and monitored with a PET scanner. 

# The PET Scanner
Below is a simple diagram for a PET scanner. 

![[Pasted image 20260318080755.png]]

The patient lies on a horizontal table and is surrounded by a ring of gamma detectors. Each detector also consists of  a photomultiplier tube and sodium iodide scintillator. Each detector is connected to a high-speed computer. 

The patient is injected with FDG. The PET scanner detects the decaying fluorine-18 nuclei positrons when they annihilate with electrons within the patient. On average a positron travels about 1mm from its emission point before it annihilates with an electron. 

Annihilation produces two gamma photons travelling in the opposite directions (due to conservation of momentum). When the computer detects the gamma photons, the computer determines the point of annihilation from the difference in the arrival times of two diametrically opposite detectors and the speed of the photons. The computer analyses and manipulates these signals to generate an image on a display screen. 

## Advantages and disadvantages of PET 
PET is a non-invasive technique (the patient is not subjected to risks of surgery). PET scans help diagnose different types of cancers, help plan complex heart surgery and help observe the function of the brain. 

This can help identify the onset of brain disorders such as Alzheimer's diseases. 

A disadvantage of PET is that is very expensive due to the facilities such as partical accelerators to produce the medical tracers. PET scannesr are only found in larger hospitals and is recommended from patients with complex health problems.
