# Potential Difference
Potential difference is the measure of the transfer of energy by charge carriers. 
### The volt 
This means that the unit of potential difference is the energy per unit charge passing through - Or in other words the **work done** per unit charge.

This means that one volt is the p.d across a component when 1 J of energy is transferred per unit charge passing through the component
$$
1V = 1JC^{-1}
$$
A p.d of 1000v means 1000J per coulomb of charge. The term potential difference used when charged particles lose energy in a component. 
**Potential difference is defined as the energy transferred from electrical energy to other forms**
$$
V = \frac{W}{Q}
$$
where $Q$ is charge in coulombs and $W$ is the energy transferred by charge Q
### Voltmeter
 An ideal voltmeter should have an infinite resistance. Used to measure p.d. They are always connected in parallel across a particular component. This is because potential difference is defined as the work done divide by the charge moved. So what the voltmeter does is that it measures the amount of energy per charge before the component and then measures the energy per charge after the current has passed through the component. This difference in energy is the voltage.

This is the reason why the voltmeter is connected in parallel - to allow measuring of energy before and after the component. 
 ![[Pasted image 20250929160137.png]]

This is why an ideal voltmeter would have an infinite resistance, this to prevent any current and thus any charge carries from flowing through the voltmeter circuit. This would be to ensure that the absolute potential difference of the component is measured. 

In reality an infinite resistance is not possible, however voltmeters with very high resistances compared to the rest of the circuit would suit just fine due to $I\propto \frac{1}{R}$:

Increasing the resistance tremendously would lead to significantly smaller amount of current flowing through the voltmeter in proportion to the the component being measured. 

$$
\begin{gather*}
\text{When R is 1 } : \\ \\
I=\frac{V}{R} \implies I \approx V \\ \\
\text{When R is large } e.g. R=9\times10^{5}, V=1 \\ \\
I=\frac{V}{R} = \frac{1}{9\times 10^{5}}= 1.111\times 10^{-6} =0.00000111\dots \\\implies I\approx 0
\end{gather*}

$$

## Electromotive Force
Potential difference describes when work is done by the charge carriers. 
Electromotive force (e.m.f) is used to describe when work is done on the charge carries. The charges have energy being transferred to them as they pass through a component like a cell, battery or power pack. The greater the e.m.f has been transferred (often from chemical energy in a cell) to it. 

**Electromotive force is defined as the energy transferred from chemical energy (or another form) to electrical energy per unit charge. **
$$
\varepsilon = \frac{W}{Q}
$$

## Calculating energy transfer
The energy transferred depends on the size of the p.d and the charge passing through the component.
$$
W = VQ
$$
or
$$
W=\varepsilon Q
$$
