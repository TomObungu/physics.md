The rate of energy transfer by each electrical component is called the electrical power (or just power). This depends on the current $I$ in the component and p.d across it.

The equation for electrical power is given by:
$$
P=VI
$$

## Why
We want to coin a term for the rate of change of energy transfer in an electrical component. This can be useful for predicting how efficient a component is based on the energy transferred over time. 

We know that voltage is the work done per unit of charge.  In order to calculate the energy transfer per unit time, we want the work done per time. 

$$
V=\frac{W}{Q}
$$

Recall that the current is the rate of flow of charge. Or in other words, the electrical charge per unit time. 

Thus multiplying the work done per unit charge gives the work done per unit time:
$$
VI=\frac{W}{\cancel{ Q} }\left( \frac{\cancel{ Q }}{t} \right)
$$
$$
\therefore \text{Rate of change over time : } \frac{W}{t}=VI
$$
$$
\therefore P=VI
$$

# Additional power
We can combine the equation above with $V=IR$ to give two additional equations for power:
$$
\begin{gather*}
P=VI \\ 
= IRI \\
=I^{2}R
\end{gather*}
$$
Rearranging $V=IR$ to make I the subject gives $I=\frac{V}{R}$. Substituting this into $P=VI$ gives.
$$
P=V\times \frac{V}{R}
= \frac{V^{2}}{R}
$$
Therefore the equations for power are:
$$
P=VI \qquad P=\frac{V^{2}}{R} \qquad P=I^{2}R \qquad P=\frac{W}{t}
$$

## Electrical energy transferred

Rearranging the equation for Power, $P=\frac{W}{t}$. It is possible to calculate the electrical energy transferred when given how long a device is used for:
$$
W=Pt
$$
Where $P$ is given in Watts.
