The pressure exerted by a gas and the mean kinetic energy of molecules of a gas are related not the mean speed of the molecules, but to the root mean square (r.m.s) speed of the molecules.

## r.m.s
The atoms or molecules of a gas move in random directions and have a range of speeds. Below is the spread in the speed of the molecules - the graph is known as the Maxwell-Boltzmann distribution.
![[Pasted image 20260107215051.png]]
Consider a container with $N$ atoms. The velocity of each atom is $c$. The random motion implies that the mean velocity of the atoms is zero.

- The mean speed = sum of speed of all the atoms divided by $N$ or simply:
$$
\bar{c} = \frac{\sum \text{c}}{N}
$$

- The mean square speed = sum of velocity$^{2}$ of all the atoms divided by $N$ or:
- $$
\bar{c^{2}} =\frac{\sum c^{2}}{N}
$$
- The root mean square speed or r.m.s speed:
-$$c_{r.m.s}=\sqrt{ \bar{c^{2}} }$$

For 4 atoms with velocities $-200ms ^{-1}$, $+ 300 ms ^{-1}$, $-430 ms ^{-1}$ and $+330 ms ^{-1}$, you can show that the mean velocity is $0 ms ^{-1}$, the mean speed is $315$ and the $r.m.s$ is $325$.

# Pressure at the microscopic level
The kinetic theory model of gases, based on Newtonian mechanics show that:
$$
pV = \frac{1}{3}N\bar{c^{2}}
$$
Where $p$ is the pressure exerted by the gas, $V$ is the volume of the gas, $N$ is the number of atoms or molecules in the gas, $m$ is the mass of each atom and $\bar{c^{2}}$ is the $r.m.s$. 


# Why molecules with larger mass have much smaller speed than air
Larger particles e.g. smoke and air molecules have the same mean kinetic energy at a specific temperature. As the mass of a smoke particle is much larger than an air molecules, according to $\bar{c^{2}} = \frac{3kT}{m}$, the $r.m.s$ speed of the more massive smoke particles is much less of the air molecules.
