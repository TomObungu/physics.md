# Pressure and Volumes
If the temperature and mass of a gas remain constant, then the pressure $p$ of an ideal gas is inversely proportional to its volume $V$. This can be expressed as:
$$
p\propto\frac{1}{V}
$$ or:
$$
\begin{gather*}
p = \frac{k}{V} \\ \
pV=k
\end{gather*}
$$
This means that:
$$
pV = \text{constant}
$$

# Pressure and temperature
If the volume and mass of gas remain constant, the pressure $p$ of an ideal gas is directly proportional to its absolute temperature $T$ in kelvin. This relationship can be expressed as:
$$
p\propto T
$$
or as:
$$
\begin{gather*}
p = kT \\ \\
\frac{p}{T} = k
\end{gather*}
$$
which can be rewritten as:
$$
\frac{p}{T} = \text{constant}
$$
Therefore:
$$
\frac{p_{1}}{T_{1}} = \frac{p_{2}}{T_{2}}
$$
For a fixed mass of gas sealed in a container, doubling the temperature will double pressure.

# Combining the gas laws
By combining the two previously described gas laws we can show that for an ideal gas:
$$
\frac{pV}{T} = \text{constant}
$$
Therefore, if the conditions are chaning from and initial state to a final state this can be written as:
$$
\frac{p_{initial}V_{inital}}{T_{total}} = \frac{p_{final}V_{final}}{T_{final}}
$$
Or simply:
$$
\frac{p_{1}V_{1}}{T_{1}} = \frac{P_{2}V_{2}}{T_{2}}
$$
# The equation state of an ideal gas
For one mole of an ideal gas, the constant in the combined relationship above is called the molar gas constant:
$$
\frac{pV}{T} = \text{constant}
$$
This constant is represented  by  $R$ is the **molar gas constant** and is $8.31JK^{-1}mol^{-1}$. This mean for $n$ moles of gas, the equation becomes:
$$
\frac{pV}{T} = nR
$$
or:
$$
pV = nRT
$$
# The relationship between pressure and the density of the gas
The quantity $M$ is known as the molar mass. It has units grams per mole. $g / mol$. It the mass of one mole of a specific substance.


If:
$$
pV=nRT
$$ And for when the number of moles, $n$ is 1, then:
$$
\begin{gather*}
m=n\times M \\
n = \frac{m}{M}
\end{gather*}
$$
If we are given the density $\rho$, it is possible to rewrite the volume in terms of the mass and density
$$
\begin{gather*}
\rho = \frac{m}{V} \\ \\
V=\frac{m}{\rho}
\end{gather*}
$$
Substituting the equations of $V$ and $n$ back into $pV=nRT$:
$$
p\left( \frac{m}{\rho} \right) = (\frac{m}{M})RT
$$
by dividing by $\frac{m}{M}$ we get:
$$
p = \frac{\rho RT}{M}
$$
This molar gas constant $R$ and Molar mass $M$ of the gas are both constant. It is possible to deduce this relationship:
$$
\begin{gather*}
p \propto \rho T \\ \\
p= k\rho T \\ \\
\frac{p}{pT} = k
\end{gather*}

$$
Or by doing the same thing again we can see that:
$$
\frac{p}{\rho T} = \text{constant}
$$Therefore knowing that pressure divided by the density multiplied by the temperature, it is possible to deduce that the initial conditions of pressure, density and Temperature are constant in same way the conditions for pressure, volume and temperature:
$$
\frac{p_{1}}{\rho_{1}T_{1}} = \frac{p_{2}}{\rho_{2}T_{2}}
$$

## Summary
Assuming the temperature is constant,
$$
p \propto  V
$$
Assuming the volume is kept constant,
$$
p \propto T
$$
Assuming the pressure is constant,
$$
V \propto T
$$
