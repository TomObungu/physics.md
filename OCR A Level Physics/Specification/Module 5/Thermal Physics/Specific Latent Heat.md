The specific latent heat of a substance, $L$, is defined as the energy required to change the phase per unit mass **while at a constant temperature**. Therefore:
$$
L = \frac{E}{m}
$$
Where $E$ is the energy supplied to change the phase of mass $m$ of the substance. There are two forms of the specific latent heat of a substance depending on the phase change. 
- When the substance changes from solid to liquid phase, this is the specific latent heat of fusion
- When the substance changes from liquid to gas phase, this is the specific latent heat of vaporisation.

In both cases the energy required to change the phase is given by:
$$
E = mL
$$
Where $m$ is the mass of the substance and $L$ is it's specific latent heat.

A thermometer should be used to ensure the ice is at its melting point, not at a lower temperature and the ice should be seen to be just starting to melt before the heater is switched on. By measuring the potential difference $V$ across the heater, the current $I$, in the heater and the time $t$ during which the heater is used, the energy transferred can be determine by:
$$
E = IVt
$$
It is important to accurately measure the mass $m$ of the substance that changes phase from solid to liquid. The specific latent heat of fusion can then be determined using:
$$
L_{f} = \frac{IVt}{m}
$$

# Determining the specific latent heat of vaporisation 
The energy required to change 1kg of substance from its liquid phase to its gaseous phase at is boiling point is often considerably more than its specific latent heat of fusion. This is due to there being a much large difference between the internal energy of a gas and a liquid than between a liquid and a solid. 

To determine L, an electrical heat can be used with a condenser to collect and then measure the mass of liquid that changes phase.

As with the specific latent heat of fusion, the specific latent heat of vaporisation can be found using:
$$
L_{v} = \frac{IVt}{m}
$$

# Combining specific latent heat and specific heat capacity 
By considering the specific heat capacity and specific latent heat of a substance, it is possible to determine the total energy required to heat and then change the phase of a substance. 

Remember when heating a solid, the kinetic energy of the solid increases but the potential energy of the solid is constant. However, during the transition of phase in melting, the potential energy of the substance increases but the kinetic energy is constant. In both cases, the internal energy increases.
