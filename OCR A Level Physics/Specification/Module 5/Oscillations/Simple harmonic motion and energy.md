For any object moving in SHM the total energy is constant, as long as there is no frictional forces. 

For a simple pendulum a graph of energy against displacement shows how the total energy of an oscillating system remains unchanged. There is a continuous interchange between potential and kinetic energy but the sum of each per unit displacement is always constant. 

![[Pasted image 20260406083908.png]]

- The kinetic energy $E_{k}$ is at a maximum when $x$ is 0
- The total energy of the oscillator must be $\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}A^{2}$
- The potential energy $E_{p}$ of the oscillator must be $\frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2}$
- The kinetic energy can be worked out by the difference in total energy and potential energy. Therefore the kinetic energy $E_{k}$ of a simple harmonic oscillator of mass m is given by:

$$
\begin{gather*}
E_{k} = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}A^{2} - \frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}x^{2} \\ \\
= E_{k} = \frac{1}{2}mv^{2} = \frac{1}{2}m\omega^{2}(A^{2}-x^{2})
\end{gather*}
$$
# Example : mass-spring system
A simple spring-mass system on a horizontal track. When displaced, the glider will move in SHM. This system has elastic potential energy and kinetic energy. The displacement $x$ of the glider is the same as the extension or compression of the spring. 

- The elastic potential energy of the system is $\frac{1}{2}kx^{2}$, where  $k$ is the spring constant
	- The elastic potential energy is always positive and varies from $E_{p}=0$, when $x=0$ to  $E_{p}=\frac{1}{2}kA^{2}$. 
	- When $x=A$, the glider will be stationary. It has no kinetic energy and the total energy is $\frac{1}{2}kA^{2}$
- The kinetic energy of the glider at any instant must be the difference between the total energy and the elastic potential energy:
$$
E_{k} = \frac{1}{2}kA^{2} -\frac{1}{2}kx^{2} = \frac{1}{2}k(A^{2}-x^{2})
$$
- The graph of $E_{k}$, against $x$ will therefore be an inverted parabola.
