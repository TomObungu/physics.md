
# Free and forced oscillations
The motion of objects released from equilibrium position which are allowed to oscillate without external forces is called free oscillation. The frequency of free oscillations is the natural frequency. 

Forced oscillation is one in which a periodic driving force is applied to an oscillator. The object will oscillate at the driving frequency. 

Oscillations are damped when an external force acts on the system which reduces the amplitude of the oscillations. 

Damping can be caused by frictional forces such as air resistance or viscous drag in oil. An example use is a car system. Without damping, the car's suspension will continue to bounce up and down every time it went over a bump.

# Light damping
This type of damping causes the amplitude of the oscillation to reduce but the period of oscillation is unchanged. The amplitude reduces exponentially. 

![[Pasted image 20260406094644.png]]

An example of light damping is a pendulum swinging in the air. 

# Heavy damping 
For this type of damping, the amplitude decreases slowly with no oscillations. 

![[Pasted image 20260406100316.png]]

An example of heavy damping is a door stopper.

# Critical Damping

In critical damping, the oscillator will return to rest at its equilibrium position in the shortest time possible without oscillating. This is the difference between critical and heavy damping.
