The sum of the resistance in a series circuit is equal to the sum of the total resistances within the circuit:
$$
R=R_{1} + R_{2} + \dots
$$
From Kirchoff's first law, we can see that the voltages will be:
$$
V=V_{1} + V_{2} + V_{3} \dots
$$
This will yield:
$$
IR = IR_{1} + IR_{2} + IR_{3} \dots
$$
However since the current is constant, we will see that:
$$
R = R_{1} + R_{2} + R_{3} \dots
$$

# Current and resistors in parallel
When resistors are connected in parallel. The total resistance drops. The additional resistor provides another path for the current, effectively increasing the cross-sectional area and so lowing the resistance,
## Why
We known that the total current is equal to the sum of the current in each resistor giving:
$$
I = I_{1} + I_{2} + I_{3}\dots
$$
For example if we have a branching in a circuit into a parallel circuits. Kirchoff's first law tells us that the sum of the current passing into a point is the same as the sum of the currents out of that point. Thus for a current of 5A into a circuit with one branch containing a 3$\Omega$ resistor and $6\Omega$ .  

The the current flowing through each branch will be in proportion to the total resistance of the resistors across the branches.
