## Equipment
- Wire
- 2 stands with claps
- 2 identical magnadur magnets (magnets that are magnetised along long face)
- Mass balance
- Ammerter
- Power pack
- Ruler

## Method
Set up the circuit and apparatus below:
![[Pasted image 20260113211436.png]]

1. Setup the wire so it is between the faces of the magnets and both awire and magnets are on top of the balance. The ammeter and power pack should be part of the complete circuit
2. Ensure there is no current flowing and that the balance has been zeroed
3. Change the supply voltage so that there is a reading of current on the ammeter to indicate there is current flowing through the wire e.g 6A
4. Record the reading on the mass balance
5. Repeat steps 3-4 and taking readings for differing values of current e.g. I = 5A, 4A, 3A,2A and 1A
6. Repeat the experiment three times 
7. Using a ruler, measure the length of the long face of magnadur magnets to determine the length of the wire in the magnetic field. 
\[Physics & Maths Tutor, 2026]


- Find the mean reading for mass for each current
- Plot a graph of mean mass against I
- Draw a line of best fit through the origin forming a straight line graph through the origin and calculate the gradient
	- The force is $F=BIL$
	- The gradient is $\frac{m}{I}$
	- The equation can be rearranged to $mg=BIL$
	- Thus $\frac{mg}{I}=BL\implies \text{gradient}\times g = BL$
	- Thefore:
	- $$
B = \frac{\text{gradient} \times g}{L}
$$
Where $L$ is in meters, $m$ in in kg and magnetic flux density $B$ is in tesla. 
\[Malmesbury Education, 2019]

## Safety
When using magnets, students with pacemakers should not be bear the magnets as they can interfere with their pacemakers function \[Physics & Maths Tutor, 2026]
