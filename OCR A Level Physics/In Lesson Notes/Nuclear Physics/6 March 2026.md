# Lesson 4 - Nuclear fusion Questions
1. Fusion happens naturally in stars like our sun. The star is so massive that gravity pushes it inwards
2. The fuel for fusion is hydrogen that is highly abundant in the core of the star. These also be isotopes of hydrogen such deuterium $^{2}_{1H}$ and tritium$^{3}_{1H}$
3.  Fusion fuel is extremely dense, it needs to be about $1.5 \times 10^{5}kgm^{-1}$ for fusion to occur. kg of fusion fuel can produce the same energy in about 10 million kg of coal. It releases more energy than fission of Uranium
4. 
   $$
\begin{gather*}
^{2}_{1}H + ^{2}_{1}H \to ^{4}_{2}He + ^{1}_{0}n\\ \\
 P \text{ (Fusion Power Density) }  \propto  \beta^{2} \text{ (Plasma beta) }  + B^{4} \text{ (Magnetic density)}
\end{gather*}
$$

5. The conditions for fusion are extremely hostile and intense. Temperatures need to be extremely high which requires a lot of energy. This is because you need to overcome the strong nuclear force. The kinetic energy needs to be at $\frac{3}{2}kT$. 
6. Not very dangerous as if the conditions are not met then nothing will occur. It also have no radioactive waste products. 
7. The electrostatic force of repulsion need to be overcome to fuse nuclei. 
8. Temperatures need to be $1.4 \times 10^{27}K$ and the density of fusion fuel is $1.5 \times 10^{5}kgm^{-1}$. 
9. Superconductors have no electrical resistance and do not heat up when current flows through. This can power extremely strong electromagnets due to having no thermal energy waste. Superconductors needs to work at $-269 ^\circ (4.15 K)$.  Takes a lot of energy to cool this. Can achieve high magnetic field and take up less space to design smaller nuclear reactions.
