## Astronomical units (AU)
From Kepler's 3rd law:
$$
\begin{gather*}
T^{2}\propto r^{3} \\ 
\frac{T^{2}}{r^{3}} = 1 \implies T^{2/3} = r
\end{gather*}
$$
This will give you the radius in AUs and the $T$ must be a proportion of 1 earth year.

If 1 Earth years means 1 Earth orbit radius  is AU. This means we can compare other planetary orbits as a number of mean Earth orbits, or AUs.

E.g. If a planet has a orbital period of 88 days. Then that is $\frac{88}{365} \approx 0.24109589041096$ years. The radius from the sun is $R = (\frac{88}{365})^{2/3} = 0.387370494909842$ AU. 

## The parsec
$$
\begin{gather*}
\tan\theta = \frac{1AU}{d} \\ \\
d = \frac{1}{p}
\end{gather*}
$$
We are limited to d being $100pc$. As the angle will be $0.01$ arcseconds which is $2..777777776\dots \times 10 ^{-6}$ degrees which is an angle too small for our current technology.

From the diagram, we can see that the distance the wave is travelling decreases as it reaches a stationary point $B$ from $A$.
$$
\begin{gather*}
c = \lambda f \\ 
c = \frac{d}{t} \\ 
t = \frac{d}{c} \\ 
\implies t \propto d \\ 
\text{As d decreases, t decreases } \\  \\
f = \frac{1}{T} \\
\text{As t decreases, frequency increase} \\ \\ 
\text{However as c is constant, as f increases } \lambda \text{ must decrease} \\ 
\end{gather*}
$$

This is the apparent observed shift in frequency as the wave emitter gets closer to us as the observer. 
# Red and Blue Shift
Doppler shift with lights means that the overall absorption and emission line wavelengths will be shifted by the same fractional shift. This is from:
$$
\frac{\Delta\lambda}{\lambda} = \frac{v}{c}
$$
However since, longer wavelengths have a more of an absolute value, the longer wavelengths will be shifted by a bigger proportion than the shorter wavelengths on the spectra lines.
