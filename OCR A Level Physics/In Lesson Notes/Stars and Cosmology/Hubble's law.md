In 1920, Edwin Hubble analysed the Doppler shift in the absorption spectra of many distinct galaxies. Using the data available he made two observations:
- The light from a vast majority of galaxies was red-shifted - they have a relative velocity away from the Earth
- In the general the further away the galaxy was, the greater the observed red shift and so the faster the galaxy was moving.
Using he formulated Hubble's law: the recessional speed $v$ of a galaxy is almost directly proportional to its distance $d$ from Earth: $v\propto d$.

# Hubble Constant
The constant of proportionality of Hubble's law is $H_{0}$:
$$
v\approx H_{0}d
$$
The SI unit for Hubble can be determine by dividing $ms^{-1}$ by $m$. This gives the unit $s^{-1}$ . However cosmologists prefer to express speed in $kms^{-1}Mpc^{-1}$ . For the exam, the Hubble constant is:
$$
H_{0} = 67.80\pm 0.77kms ^{-1}Mpc ^{-1} \qquad H_{0} = 2.2 \times 10 ^{-18} s ^{-1}
$$
# The cosmological principle
The cosmological principle is the assumption that, when viewed on a large enough scale, the Universe is homogeneous and isotropic.

The laws of physics can be applied across the Universe. The theories and models tested here on the Earth can be applied to everything within the Universe over all time and space.

- Homoegenous - Matter is distributed unifromly accross the Universe. For a very large voulme, the density of the universe if uniform. This means the same type of structures (galaxies) are seen everywhere
- Isotropic - The universe looks the same in all directions to every observer. It follows that there is no centre or edge to the universe. The universe would look the same wherever you are.
